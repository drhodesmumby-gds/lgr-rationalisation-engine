Cost Approach 1: System-level split (simplest)

One cost split for the entire system, weighted by how many functions each successor participates in.

Pro: Simple — one number per successor.
Con: South only uses Payroll but pays 20% of the whole system. Is that fair?
Cost Approach 2: Per-sharing-group (derived from grid)

The grid creates distinct "sharing groups" — each group gets its own cost allocation. System cost is first divided across functions, then each function's cost is split among its participants.

Default: simple system-level split. Optional: expand to override per-function if needed. Progressive disclosure.

Pro: Simple by default, powerful when needed. Progressive disclosure matches persona needs — Executive sees top-level, Architect drills in.
Con: System-level default might be "wrong enough" that users don't know to drill in. Needs smart auto-calculation to be useful at the top level.

Domain note: In practice, most council contracts are system-level (one contract = one cost, regardless of how many functions it serves). Per-function cost allocation is an internal modelling exercise, not a contractual reality. This suggests Approach 1 or 3 — system-level reflects how costs actually land, with per-function breakdown being analytical rather than operational.
